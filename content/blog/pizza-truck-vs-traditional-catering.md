**Short version:** a pizza truck wins at outdoor, informal, long-running events at venues without a kitchen. Traditional catering wins at formal, seated, indoor events and anywhere a single coordinated service moment matters. Most people know which category they're in within one sentence of reading that.

Here's the longer comparison, including the cases where each is genuinely the wrong call.

## Where a pizza truck wins

### Venues with no kitchen

This is the decisive one. Barns, farms, vineyards, backyards, rooftops, lofts, beaches. Our truck is fully self-contained — the oven runs on its own fuel and needs no power, gas or water from the site.

Traditional catering at those venues means building a temporary kitchen, and that cost frequently exceeds the food.

### Events where people eat at different times

A graduation party where guests arrive across three hours. A block party. A wedding where the dancing matters more than the dinner. The truck fires continuously, so there's no "last tray" moment and no cold food sitting in a chafing dish.

### When you want people to remember the food

A truck is the thing guests photograph and talk about afterward. That's not a small consideration at a wedding or a client event, and it's why the truck often outperforms a more expensive plated option on the metric that actually matters to the host.

### Long service windows

Because pies come out every ninety seconds for as long as you've booked, a four-hour party doesn't need four hours of food held warm. It needs four hours of cooking.

## Where traditional catering wins

### Seated, formal dinners

If people are sitting at assigned tables expecting courses, a truck is the wrong instrument. Book traditional catering.

### Fully indoor venues with no outdoor access

The truck serves from outside — a driveway, lot, curbside, courtyard or lawn. If there's genuinely nowhere outside for it, trays or full service are the answer. (For some indoor venues we can discuss a portable oven setup, but it's a conversation, not a default.)

### Dense Manhattan addresses

Plenty of Manhattan streets have nowhere legal for a truck to sit. We'll tell you honestly when that's your situation rather than take the booking and improvise. [Drop-off or staffed catering](/catering/manhattan) works everywhere the truck doesn't.

### Wide-ranging menus

If your guest list needs six distinct main options, trays and full service flex further than an oven does.

### Breakfast and early-morning events

The truck is a lunch-through-late-night tool. Office breakfast catering is a tray job.

## Cost, compared honestly

Pizza truck catering starts at **$1,500**, and scales with guest count, service length and travel.

Traditional catering splits into drop-off trays (the cheapest real option, no staffing) and full service (servers and bartenders, priced largely on staff hours). Tray pricing is published on our [catering menu](/catering).

For a 100-guest outdoor party, the truck usually lands between drop-off and full service — and it removes the kitchen build, the cleanup and the person who otherwise spends the party plating. For a 40-guest office lunch, drop-off trays win on cost every time.

The comparison that misleads people is truck-versus-drop-off on food cost alone. Include the venue's kitchen requirements and the labour, or you're not comparing the same thing.

## You can have both

This is worth saying because people treat it as either/or. At weddings especially, the most common booking we take is traditional catering for the meal and the [pizza truck for late night](/pizza-trucks/weddings) — the truck arrives around ten, fires for an hour, and rescues everyone who's been dancing since eight.

Cocktail hour works the same way. The oven's hot when guests arrive, so nobody's standing at a dry bar waiting for photographs to finish.

## A three-question test

1. **Is there somewhere outside for a truck roughly the size of a parking space?** If no, book traditional catering.
2. **Are people seated for courses?** If yes, book traditional catering.
3. **Does the venue have a working kitchen?** If no, the truck just saved you a kitchen build.
