Catering for 100 people in New York splits into two very different jobs, and that is the reason every online estimate you find is useless. **Drop-off tray catering and full-service catering are not the same product**, and quoting them as one range is how people end up shocked by a number.

Here's how to work out which one you need, and what actually drives the cost.

## Drop-off catering: the cheapest real option

We deliver hot trays, salads and party heros. You set them out. There's no staffing, no service charge, and no cleanup crew.

For 100 people you're generally looking at a combination of hot trays by the full pan, a tossed salad or two, side salads by the pound, and party heros by the foot. Our current tray pricing is on the [catering menu](/catering) — it's listed openly rather than hidden behind a form.

This is the right answer for graduation parties, communions, office lunches, backyard birthdays, and most family gatherings. It's what the majority of our 100-guest bookings are.

## Full-service catering: a different job

Servers, bartenders, setup, service through the event, and full breakdown. The food cost is a smaller share of the total than people expect — you're largely paying for staff hours.

This is the right answer for weddings, client dinners, launch events, and anything where the room needs to feel considered rather than fed.

## The four things that move the number

### Staffing

The single biggest variable in full-service catering, and a non-factor in drop-off. More service hours, more staff, higher cost. It's that direct.

### Menu

Hot trays are the efficient choice at scale. Passed hors d'oeuvres and plated courses cost more per head because they cost more in labour, not just ingredients.

### Venue

A venue with a working kitchen is cheaper to cater than one without. A barn, a loft, a backyard or a rooftop means we bring equipment that a hotel would have supplied. Ask any caterer what your venue changes about their quote — if they say "nothing," be careful.

### Access

This one is specific to New York and it's real. A fourth-floor walk-up in Brooklyn, a Manhattan building with a strict freight-elevator window, a block with no legal place to stop — each of those changes how many people we send and how long it takes.

We ask about it before we quote. A caterer who doesn't ask is going to find out on the day, and that's when things go wrong.

## Add a bar and the maths changes again

If you want alcohol, there are two routes:

- **Dry hire** — you buy the alcohol, we bring TIPS-certified bartenders, the full bar setup, mixers, ice, garnishes and glassware. From **$22 per person**.
- **Open bar** — we supply the alcohol too. From **$26 per person** for beer and wine, up to $62 for ultra-premium.

Both include liquor liability insurance and the Caterer's Alcohol Permit, which we handle. See [mobile bar packages](/mobile-bar) or [hiring bartenders](/mobile-bar/bartenders).

## A cheaper option people overlook

For 100 guests at an outdoor or informal event, a [pizza truck](/pizza-trucks) is often less than full-service catering and more memorable than trays. It starts at $1,500, fires continuously so nobody runs out, and needs no kitchen at the venue at all.

It doesn't suit every event. For a formal dinner it's the wrong tool. For a graduation party, a block party or a backyard wedding, it frequently beats both alternatives on cost and on how the day actually feels.
